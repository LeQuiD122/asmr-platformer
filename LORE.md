# Harrow Bay: the Hold

The story of the game, as canon. Everything the townsfolk say (`Townsfolk.LINES`), every notice board
(`StoryService.NOTES`), every keepsake (`Shared/Keepsakes.lua`) and every caption comes from here.
When a level, a person or a material is added, it gets its place in this document first, and
`check_story.py` holds the code to the parts of it that can be checked.

Tagged the way `ROADMAP.md` is: **(you)** for your ideas, **(suggested)** for mine.

**The screenplay** is `story/HARROW_BAY.fountain`: the whole game written as a film, scene by scene and
shot by shot, in the order it happens, with notes saying which code plays each scene. Open it in any
Fountain app (Beat, Highland, Fade In) or read it as plain text. When a scene in the game and the
screenplay disagree, one of them is wrong; decide which, and fix it.

## The one idea

**Everything you stand on was in somebody's hands when the water came.**

The game is a platformer made of materials you want to press: honey, soap, bubble wrap, slime, sand,
keypads. The story is why. In Harrow Bay the tide does not carry things away. It keeps them. Whatever
anyone was holding, squeezing, pressing or fidgeting with in the last hours before the gates stayed
shut was kept by the water and grown into the stepping stones the levels are made of. Every surface
wants to be pressed because it was being pressed when the day stopped.

The idea that the people are living an ordinary day inside what the player calls the backrooms is
**(you)**. The Hold, the keepsakes and everything below that grows out of them are **(suggested)**.

## What happened

- **1911.** The Harrow Bay Corporation Waterworks opens: an engine house, a pump hall and three
  outfall gates under the harbour, with the Corporation Baths built underneath and warmed by the
  engines' waste heat. Outfalls 1 and 2 can be opened by hand. Outfall 3, the big one, has a lock
  and one key, and the key goes home with whoever is on call: the key holder.
- **The key holders.** J. Harrow, 1911. A. Wick, 1946 (the fisherman on the pier now; "gave it back
  on time"). E. Marsh, 1979 ("did not come back up"). And you, 14 August.
- **11 August.** A surge comes in on the high tide. Mr Barlow, the night engineer, opens Outfalls 1
  and 2 by hand and puts every light in the station on so the key holder can find the way in. The key
  holder does not come. The tide does not go out. The Promenade Hotel opens its roof to everyone and
  the party starts.
- **12 and 13 August.** High water at 11:52 every night. The clocks above the station start stopping
  at 11:52 and do not start again. On the night of the 13th the key holder takes the key home to Hill
  House.
- **14 August.** The day the Hold keeps.
  - 9:12. Sal drops a jar of honey on the Front steps.
  - 9:14. Sal's first customer since this morning. Pip is building a sandcastle. Tobi Okafor goes
    down to find the key man, in his trunks, with his red towel. (Level 1.)
  - 12:00. The siren. The council says it is only a test.
  - 15:00. The party on the roof. Mrs Okafor keeps Tobi's seat. Dev is still in the post office queue
    with a parcel, somewhere, forever. (Level 2.)
  - 23:52. Every clock in town stops. The fisherman watches people go down the harbour drain looking
    for the key man. None come back up. (Level 3.)
  - 23:56. Under the pumping station, in the baths, the clocks are moving again. (Level 4.)
  - 00:00. The gates. The key was in your pocket. The tide goes out.
- **And then 9:14 again.**

## The Hold

The old-timers' word for it: Mr Wick, Mrs Venn, Mr Barlow. The water is held behind the gates, and
the day is held with it. Nobody who lives in the Hold says "backrooms". People from up there do, and
Mrs Venn has an answer for them: it is the back rooms of the baths, dear. Staff only.

**Its rules**, which everything new has to keep to:

- **It is always 14 August.** Time runs forward inside a level, later in each level, and the day
  starts again at 9:14 when the gates open.
- **The only way on is down.** Every level ends by going down: the high dive, the slide, the drain,
  the flume. Nobody has found a way up. The Promenade Hotel's stairs only go down to the street, and
  the street is not there any more.
- **It keeps what was held.** Every platform is a keepsake (below).
- **It remembers your weight, and then forgets it.** Footprints, dents, cracks, holes: the Hold
  holds them for a while, then fills them in, because the day always comes round again. This is the
  game's "nothing stays broken" (HANDOFF) given a reason.
- **What was held too tight gives way; what was held gently holds on.** The risk materials break
  under you (bubble wrap, soap, ice, sand, salt); the pace materials slow you down and let you go
  (honey, butter, foam, the keyboard).
- **The water only ever rises**, until the gates.
- **Clocks stop at 11:52 above the pumping station and move below it.**
- **The siren at noon is a test. The siren at midnight is not.**
- **The people repeat their day and half recognise you.** They do not leave their places. The ones
  who went down to look for the key holder are in the baths, still looking.
- **A key holder the Hold keeps too long loses their face.** Nobody says so. E. Marsh stands on a
  ledge of the Deco tower and watches the route, and has never said a word.

**What keeps it.** Something is at the bottom of the Hold. Mr Wick has felt it pass under the street
("It isn't hungry. It's patient."); the Sunken City's long shape in the water is some part of it; Mr
Barlow warns you that what is at the bottom of the flume is not the water; Mrs Venn does not look under
the loose board. At the end of the fall it opens its mouth, and it is only ever its mouth: rows of long
thin teeth, a glow far down the throat, the rest of it the dark. It shuts on you, and you come up in the
Gate Chamber as if you had been given back. The last thing it says, on a black screen, is what it is:
**The Hold. It keeps what it is given.** It is never named, never seen whole and never explained **(the
mouth is you; the rest suggested)**.
## You

The key holder for Outfall 3. The time card in the pumping station has your name on it and says NOT
CLOCKED IN; so does the rota under the baths. Everyone half recognises you ("You've got his face").
The key was in your pocket all along. You took it home to Hill House on the 13th, and a front door
key on a green ribbon, marked HILL HOUSE, is in the baths' lost property.

**Why you did not come in is never said.** It is the question the story is built round, kept for a
level of its own (below).

## How the story is told

In layers, the way environmental storytelling works (Smith and Worch, "What Happened Here?"): nothing
is ever told outright, and every layer rewards the player who went looking in the one before.

- **The words that open each level**: where, when, and one line of what the town would call the news.
- **The people**, who greet you and have more to say each time you talk to them (F), and who think it
  is an ordinary day.
- **The keepsakes**: the first time you stand on each material, a card says whose it was.
- **Things to read**: notice boards (R), and letters lying on the ground, a stone on each.
- **Things to open**: lockers, a tin box, loose bricks, a loose board. Some open with a hand (E); some
  only to MR BARLOW'S PRY BAR, which lies somewhere early on every level and stays on your back.
- **The secrets**: five, one or more a level, each told as a short scene when it is found, the camera
  drifting in on it with the focus pulled to it, a low sting, its name and a line, then "Secret 2 of 5"
  and what it says. Not needed to finish anything; the whole story is only in them.
- **The endings**: every level ends going down, played as a scene you do not control, and the level's
  own sound goes silent when it starts. Only the scene's sound is left.
- **The sound**: every level has its own bed (the sea, the roof wind and the party through a wall, the
  harbour at night, the baths' hum), and under all of them the same low drone: the Hold. It is only
  noticed when it stops, and it stops at every ending.

## The people

- **Sal** sells ice cream on the Front. Dropped the honey. Says the pump man is on his way; knows the
  key holder took the key home.
- **Pip Okafor**, eight. Waiting for her dad by her sandcastle. Knows the sand forgets footprints and
  that she does not.
- **Maren**, the lifeguard on the dive deck. Knows you are from the station, and that you are late.
- **Dev** runs the rooftop bar. Keeps the guest list. You are on the other list.
- **Mrs Ada Okafor** keeps Tobi's seat on a lounger. It is always nearly three.
- **Rudy** plays the last song, which is the first song, four hundred times.
- **The Attendant** at the aquarium. Asks you not to tap the glass. Runs when it goes.
- **Mr Wick**, the fisherman. Held the key himself in 1946. Knows the word for the Hold, and that
  nobody who goes down the drain comes back.
- **The Watcher**, faceless, on the Deco tower. E. Marsh, key holder from 1979. Never speaks.
- **Mrs Venn**, the baths attendant. Has sold a ticket to everyone who came down looking for you.
  Cuts the soap at nine; it comes back uncut at nine.
- **Tobi Okafor**, Pip's dad, in his trunks with his red towel. Still looking. The halls are longer
  every time.
- **R. Pell**, the Siren Keeper, never seen. Has sounded the siren every midnight since the 11th and
  left a letter behind the bricks in the Siren Room.
- **Mr Barlow**, the night engineer. Has been on since the 11th. Can start the engines, cannot open
  the gates, knows what the Hold is made of and what happened to Marsh.

## The keepsakes

One for every material, in `Shared/Keepsakes.lua`, which is the source of truth: what it was, where
it came from, and one line about who was holding it. The first time you stand on each material in a
session a card says so: "Keepsake 5 of 27". By place:

- **The Front:** Sal's honey, the dairy cart's butter, Pip's sandcastle sand, the Corporation Gardens'
  lamb's ear, St Brigid's candle.
- **Mill Lane, the Novelty Works:** slime, the squeeze toys, and Hale's honey from the hives on its roof.
- **The High Street:** Marlow's chocolate (two kinds), Marlow's Toys' bricks, Dev's parcel in the post
  office queue.
- **Quay Street and the harbour:** the fishmonger's ice, the Ice House snow, the salt pans, the harbour
  mud, the aquarium's jellyfish.
- **The Promenade Hotel:** the rooftop bar's soda jelly, Room 14's mattress, the laundry steam.
- **The Waterworks:** the Control Room's teleprinter, pump panel and switchboard; the Engine House's
  coal, clinker and gauge keys; the Brickworks clay its walls were pressed from; Mrs Venn's soap.

## Harrow Bay

The Front and its sands; the pier; the Promenade Hotel; the High Street (Marlow's sweet shop,
Marlow's Toys, the post office); Quay Street (the fishmonger, the Ice House); Mill Lane (the Novelty
Works, Hale's hives); the Brickworks; the salt pans past the pier; the Corporation Gardens and the
bandstand; the aquarium; St Brigid's on the hill; Hill House, with the big clock; and the Harrow Bay
Corporation Waterworks: the Control Room, the Engine House, the pump hall, Outfalls 1 to 3, the
Corporation Baths underneath, and the Gate Chamber at the very bottom.

## What is never explained

- Why you did not come in.
- Whether opening the gates ends the day or only starts it again.
- Who the Watcher was, beyond a name on a list.
- What the lobby is. (It is the one place with no water and no time of day, which may be the answer.)
- What is at the bottom of the Hold, beyond its mouth.
- How the Outfall 3 key is in your pocket at midnight when you left it on its hook at Hill House.

## Hooks for later (suggested)

- **Level 5: Hill House, 8:00 in the morning.** Before any of it: your house on the hill, the big
  clock, the key on its green ribbon on the hook by the door, and the reason you did not go. The
  true ending would be clocking in.
- **A keepsake journal**, saved between sessions and shown in the lobby: 27 cards to find.
- **The radio on the roof**: Rudy's song broken into by the shipping forecast for Harrow Bay, the same
  forecast every time.
- **Marsh's ledge**: a way up to it that nobody would think to look for, and a note in his handwriting.

## Writing rules

- No dashes of any kind in anything a player reads (`check_story.py` fails on one).
- The townsfolk speak plainly and politely, like a British seaside town. One idea per line. After the
  first line (the greeting), every line has to work on its own, in any order.
- Eerie by understatement. Nobody panics except the Attendant when the glass goes. Nobody explains
  the Hold; the old-timers name it and that is all.
- Every new material needs a keepsake, every new person needs a look and at least three lines, and
  at least two people on every level carry the key holder's thread. `check_story.py` fails otherwise.
- Every level's things to read include at least one that could only be from that place.
